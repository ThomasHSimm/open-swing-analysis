# Barzyk & Gruber 2024 — Motor learning in golf (systematic review)

**Register tier:** Background / methods-watch only — *not* a design-parameter source. Construct mismatch is total (motor-learning pedagogy, not balance/CoP). Kept for one reason: attentional-focus cueing is a confound the trial may introduce itself.

**Citation**: Barzyk P, Gruber M. Motor learning in golf — a systematic review. *Front Sports Act Living* 6:1324615, 2024.

**DOI**: 10.3389/fspor.2024.1324615

**Study type**: Systematic review of RCTs (PRISMA, RoB 2). 52 studies.

**Population**: Across studies — predominantly novices (41/52) performing putting (43/52). Mixed ages incl. children and elderly.

**Intervention**: N/A. Reviewed strategies: focus of attention, augmented feedback, practice scheduling, implicit/explicit learning, cognitive training.

**Comparator**: Per PICO — no-learning or different-learning control.

**Duration**: Highly variable (single session to 10 weeks; ~20 to 3,000 trials). No standardisation.

**Outcome measures**: Putting/chipping accuracy (absolute/radial/variable error); a few full-swing studies use carry/total distance. **No CoP, GRF, or weight-transfer kinematics.**

**Key findings**: "Superior" methods within strategies — external focus of attention, increasing contextual interference, distributed practice, errorless learning. Augmented feedback and cognitive training inconclusive.

| Strategy | n | Mean Cohen's f | Mean power |
|---|---|---|---|
| Augmented feedback | 11 | 0.61 | 0.89 |
| Focus of attention | 12 | 0.54 | 0.64 |
| Practice scheduling | 6 | 0.50 | 0.70 |
| Implicit/explicit | 11 | 0.45 | 0.60 |
| Cognitive training | 12 | 0.44 | 0.63 |

(f: 0.1 small / 0.25 medium / 0.4 large. Only augmented feedback clears 0.8 power as a group.)

**Statistical approach**: RoB 2; post-hoc power analysis (G*Power); effect sizes as Cohen's f.

**Relevance to trial design**:
- *Outcome measures*: none transferable. Putting accuracy ≠ CoP. Do not draw outcome precedent here.
- *Training duration*: weakly informative — no consensus, and these are skill-acquisition not strength/balance-adaptation protocols.
- *Expected effect sizes*: wrong construct; do not use these f values to power a CoP study. Low confidence in any read-across.
- *Confounder (the real takeaway)*: if the trial uses any verbal cue ("feel your weight shift" = internal vs "drive the clubhead" = external), focus of attention is a documented moderator of golf-task outcomes. An uncontrolled cue could change movement/CoP independent of the balance intervention. Standardise and report cueing.

**Limitations noted**: >half (29/52) underpowered; heterogeneous populations and practice volumes; novice/putting dominance; mostly studies *through* golf, not *for* golf (low ecological validity).

**Quality assessment**: Medium–high as a review of its own field; **Low for this trial** (construct mismatch).

**Direct quotes worth keeping**: none essential; paraphrase.

**Flags / contradictions**:
1. Construct mismatch is the headline — motor-learning pedagogy, not biomechanics/balance. Only bridge is the cueing confound.
2. Full-swing / advanced-golfer gap: review states novice-putting findings may not transfer to full swing or skilled players, and that distance is not a clean performance proxy. Directly limits read-across if the pilot uses non-novices hitting full shots.
3. Methodological: post-hoc (observed) power is widely criticised as a monotonic transform of the p-value; using it to label studies "underpowered" after the fact is contentious. Their underlying small-sample point is fair, but do not cite their power figures as prospective. (Medium-high confidence — standard statistical objection.)
4. Minor table errors: Table 3 mislabels Bieńkiewicz as ref (15) (that is Bahmani; Bieńkiewicz is 82). Guadagnoli (37) "HCP between −7 and −16" is nonsensical as written — likely OCR/source error.
