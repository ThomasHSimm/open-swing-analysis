# You et al. (2023) — GRF, Foot Positions & Club Type in Golf (Network Meta-Analysis)

> **IDENTITY FLAG — read first:** This is NOT priority paper #2. The priority list's GRF systematic review is a *Sports Medicine* 2025 paper (DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02391-3). This is a separate, earlier, lower-tier review: You et al. 2023, *Applied Sciences*. Already cited inside the Yang 2024 extraction. If you intended to process the Sports Medicine review, this isn't it.
>
> **TRIAGE:** Low-quality network meta-analysis with serious methodological problems (see Quality + Contradiction). Entirely GRF *magnitude*, no timing — least useful paper in the set for a timing-based design. Recommend NOT a full evidence slot; keep as a pointer to its primary sources (Severin 2021 for trial reliability/variability; Peterson 2016/2019 for impulse/balance regulation).

## Citation
You X, Xu Y, Liang M, Baker JS, Gu Y. The Relationship between Ground Reaction Forces, Foot Positions and Type of Clubs Used in Golf: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. *Applied Sciences.* 2023;13(12):7209.

## DOI
10.3390/app13127209 (Open Access, CC BY; MDPI)

## Study type
Systematic review + **network (mixed-treatment) meta-analysis** treating club types as "interventions." Underlying primary studies are cross-sectional/case-control, despite the protocol claiming RCT-only inclusion (see Contradiction).

## Population
7 studies, 422 participants total (inflated by Chu 2010, n=308; remaining analysable studies n=8–63). Adults, both sexes, recreational to professional, ~21–60 yr, height ~1.7–1.9 m, mass ~75–84 kg, 5–10 swings each. Included: Han 2019 (n=63), Severin 2021 (n=10), Worsfold 2007 (n=8), Peterson 2019 (n=9), Peterson 2016 (n=11), Chu 2010 (n=308), Gatt 1998 (n=13).

## Intervention
**Club type** treated as the "intervention": driver, iron 3, iron 5, iron 6, iron 7, pitching wedge (PW). Not a training intervention.

## Comparator
Driver as the common comparator; indirect club-vs-club comparisons via the network.

## Duration
**N/A** — single-session biomechanics across pooled studies. No training/duration information.

## Outcome measures
Standard 3D GRF taxonomy, normalised to body weight, split by foot:
- **Per foot:** trail vs lead.
- **Per plane:** vertical (Z), mediolateral (X), anteroposterior (Y).
- Six pooled outcomes: TVGRF, TMGRF, TAGRF, LVGRF, LMGRF, LAGRF. Plus resultant horizontal force (RFH).
*All magnitude. No temporal/timing metrics, no rate-of-change, no per-event CoP.*

## Key findings
- **Consistent, credible:** the **lead foot generates larger GRF than the trail foot**, especially vertical (corroborates McHugh, Ball, Okuda). E.g. Han 2019 lead Z ≈ 1.12–1.16 BW vs trail Z ≈ 0.79; Chu 2010 lead Z 0.951 vs trail Z 0.645; Gatt 1998 lead RFH 0.999 vs trail 0.715.
- **Driver produces greater RFH than irons** in both feet; RFH matters for drive distance.
- **Club type changes GRF** (attributed to club length/weight altering stance and leg stiffness): more upright stance with short irons → more even lead/trail distribution; longer clubs (driver) → more lead-foot loading.
- **Per-outcome "best club" rankings** (e.g. "iron 7 best for TMGRF," "iron 6 best for TVGRF") — DISREGARD as actionable (see Contradiction; rest on an unjustified "lower = better" premise).

## Statistical approach
Network meta-analysis via ADDIS (Bayesian, ADDIS V1.16.8); evidence structure/inconsistency checked (CINeMA, consistency vs inconsistency random-effects SDs compared). Data BW-normalised in Excel. Risk of bias: NHLBI tool (case-control). Only 3/7 studies entered the quantitative NMA; the rest narratively reviewed.

## Relevance to trial design
- **Training duration:** None.
- **Appropriate outcome measures:** Useful only as confirmation of the **standard 3D per-foot GRF taxonomy** (trail/lead × X/Y/Z + RFH) — a reasonable vocabulary for reporting your kinetic outcomes. But it does NOT characterise *timing* or *natural variability*, which is the part of "standard outcomes + variability" your priority-2 paper is meant to supply. For variability/trial-count, go to the included primary source **Severin et al. 2021** ("The required number of trials for biomechanical analysis of a golf swing") directly.
- **Expected effect sizes:** None usable — NMA reports rank probabilities, not interpretable effect sizes, and the underlying data are tiny/low-quality.
- **Likely confounders — one genuinely useful design note:** the review's defensible conclusion is that **club type should be treated as a confounding variable** in golf GRF research. Directly relevant: if your intervention uses irons while your comparison literature (e.g. Yang, the cross-sectional GRF studies) uses drivers, club type confounds cross-study comparison. Standardise and report the club used.

## Limitations noted by authors
Small samples, low study quality (no 'good' ratings), limited high-quality GRF evidence, environmental heterogeneity across studies, narrow club coverage. Authors repeatedly caution the meta-analysis quality is "relatively low."

## Quality assessment
**Low.** Beyond the authors' own caveats:
- **Protocol contradiction:** Methods claim RCT-only inclusion; all included studies are observational/case-control (their own quality table confirms). Undermines confidence in the review's conduct.
- **NMA mis-specification:** club types are not exchangeable "treatments"; transitivity assumption not credibly met across heterogeneous tiny studies.
- **Unjustified value framing:** "lower GRF = better" is asserted without performance linkage, making all club rankings uninterpretable.
- **No good-quality primary studies** (3 poor, 4 fair).
- **Editorial red flags:** garbled physics in the text ("Newton's third constitution of movement"; "every deed, there is an identical and facing response") suggest weak review/translation rigour.
Net: treat conclusions as hypothesis-generating at best.

## Direct quotes worth keeping
Prefer paraphrase. The one defensible, citable design takeaway:
- Future biomechanical research should treat golf club type as a confounding variable when studying GRFs during swings.
(Avoid quoting the "lower = better" rankings or the garbled mechanics.)

## Contradiction flag
**Not a contradiction of your design so much as a paper to handle with caution.**
1. **Internal contradiction (use against citing it as strong evidence):** the RCT-only inclusion claim is false against its own included studies — cite this paper only for the uncontroversial lead>trail GRF point and the club-as-confounder note, never as a quality evidence base.
2. **Wrong dimension for your study:** entirely magnitude; the skill-discriminating signal (per McHugh, Ball) is timing/unweighting, which this review does not address. Don't let it pull your outcome selection back toward magnitude.
3. **"Lower GRF = better" is the trap:** if any of your rationale cites club/GRF "optimisation," do not inherit this premise. There is no established direction in which mediolateral or anteroposterior GRF magnitude is "better" absent a performance model.
4. **Redirect:** the real value is upstream — pull **Severin et al. 2021** (trial reliability/variability — matches your priority-2 intent) and **Peterson et al. 2016/2019** (angular impulse and balance regulation during the swing — on-construct for weight-transfer kinetics) as primary sources rather than relying on this synthesis.
