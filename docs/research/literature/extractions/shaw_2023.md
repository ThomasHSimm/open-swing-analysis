# Shaw et al. 2023 — TrackMan within/between-session reliability

**Register tier:** Evidence — *measurement layer only* (between-session TrackMan reliability for ball-flight metrics, if TrackMan is used). Not relevant to CoP/balance.

**Citation**: Shaw J, Gould ZI, Oliver JL, Lloyd RS. Within- and between-session reliability of golf swing variables using the TrackMan launch monitor in talented golfers. *J Strength Cond Res* 37(12):2431–2437, 2023.

**DOI**: not printed in the PDF — to be sourced.

**Study type**: Test–retest reliability study (within-subject repeated-measures). Not an intervention.

**Population**: n=21 (16 M, 5 F), age 18.3 ± 3.3 y, national-pathway golfers, ≥4 y experience, ~4 sessions/week. See flagged inconsistency on handicap below.

**Intervention**: None.

**Comparator**: None (reliability design).

**Duration**: 2 sessions ≥7 days apart; 3 shots per club (6-iron, driver) per session.

**Outcome measures**: CHS, ball speed, smash factor, attack angle, club path, launch angle, spin rate, spin axis, carry distance, total distance — all TrackMan doppler radar. Reliability via rm-ANOVA (within), paired t-tests (between), SEM, CV%, ICC with 95% CIs.

**Key findings**:
- Reliable both within and between session: CHS, ball speed, carry, total distance (CV <10%, good–excellent ICC). CHS most reliable (ICC 0.98–0.99; CV 0.7–1.3%).
- Launch angle reliable for 6-iron; spin rate reliable between-session for 6-iron only.
- Attack angle, club path, spin rate, spin axis: high random variation — but see ICC caveat in flags.
- No systematic bias trial-to-trial or session-to-session; authors argue no familiarisation needed (in this talented sample).
- Detectable change thresholds: ~1% (6-iron) / 0.74% (driver) CHS, vs a cited ~4.1% mean CHS gain from 6–18 wk resistance training.

**Statistical approach**: Shapiro–Wilk, Mauchly; Bonferroni post hoc; Koo & Li ICC bands; CV <10% acceptable.

**Relevance to trial design**:
- *Outcome measures*: useful only if ball flight is a SECONDARY outcome. CHS/ball speed/carry/total are reliable enough for pre/post on TrackMan. Says nothing about CoP — the primary construct.
- *Detecting change*: gives between-session detectable thresholds for TrackMan ball-flight metrics.
- *Confounders*: keep club/ball/fitting constant across sessions; centeredness of strike drives launch-angle reliability.
- *Training duration / effect sizes / balance*: N/A.

**Limitations noted**: n=21, 2 sessions only; only 2 of up to 14 clubs; generalisability to higher handicaps limited.

**Quality assessment**: Medium for its own purpose; **Low for this trial** (wrong instrument and construct — ball flight, not CoP).

**Direct quotes worth keeping**: none essential; paraphrase.

**Flags / contradictions**:
1. Internal inconsistency: paper labels the sample "category 1 (handicap 5 or less)" yet reports mean handicap 11.79 ± 2.35 and repeats "mean handicap of 11.8." Both cannot be true; the category label or the handicap is wrong.
2. The "unreliable" angular metrics are partly a CV artefact (means near zero inflate CV%); ICC tells a different story — attack angle has good ICC (0.83–0.90) despite >100% CV, whereas spin axis is genuinely poor (ICC 0.17–0.33). Judge these by ICC/SEM, not CV. The paper's blanket "use with caution" lumps the two together.
3. OCR note: table values shown as "20.02", "20.20" etc. are negative numbers (−0.02, −0.20).
