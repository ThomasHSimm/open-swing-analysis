# Li et al. 2024 — TOGU Training Golf

**Citation**: Li, M.; Ruan, C.; Zhang, L. Impact of Four Weeks of TOGU Training on Neuromuscular Control and Golf Swing Performance. *J. Funct. Morphol. Kinesiol.* 2024, 9, 243.

**DOI**: 10.3390/jfmk9040243

**Study type**: RCT (two-group, pre-post; randomisation procedure not described)

**Population**: n=29 right-handed male golfers, handicap 15–20 (sub-elite). Age 21.06 ± 1.06 years, height 175.19 ± 5.14 cm, weight 72.11 ± 10.04 kg. TOGU group n=16, control n=13.

**Intervention**: TOGU Balanza + Dynair Ballkissen equipment. 3 sessions/week × 4 weeks, 60 min/session including 10-min warm-up. Week 1: floor-based core work (plank, Russian twist, back extensions, kneeling disc plank). Weeks 2–4: progressively unstable unilateral and bilateral standing exercises on balance disc and cushion, including medicine ball throws, rope rotations, and power stick swings. All sessions in addition to regular golf skill training (2×/week).

**Comparator**: Control group — regular golf skill training only, 2×/week × 4 weeks. No TOGU component.

**Duration**: 4 weeks. TOGU group: 3×/week additional training + 2×/week golf. Control: 2×/week golf only. Note the training volume difference is a confound — groups differ in total weekly training time.

**Outcome measures**:
- FMS (7 subtests: deep squat, hurdle step, in-line lunge, shoulder mobility, ASLR, trunk stability push-up, rotary stability; 0–3 scale each, max 21)
- mCTSIB via NeuroCom Balance Master: COG sway velocity (deg/s) under 4 conditions — Firm-EO, Firm-EC, Foam-EO, Foam-EC
- UST via NeuroCom Balance Master: COG sway velocity (deg/s) — L-EO, L-EC, R-EO, R-EC
- GCQuad launch monitor: club head speed (m/s), ball speed (m/s), driving distance (yards/metres), hitting efficiency (ball speed / club head speed)

**Key findings**:

*FMS*: TOGU group total score improved significantly (p<0.001, +11.48%). Deep squat (p<0.05, +23%) and right in-line lunge (p<0.05, +17.6%) reached significance. Left in-line lunge borderline (p=0.051). Trunk stability push-up and shoulder mobility did not improve. Control: no significant changes on any subtest.

*Balance (mCTSIB)*: Significant reductions in sway velocity for TOGU group — Foam-EO −30.9% (p<0.01), Firm-EC −35.2% (p<0.05), Foam-EC −36.8% (p<0.001). Firm-EO not reported as significant (likely ceiling — already good with vision on firm surface). Control: no changes.

*Balance (UST)*: L-EO −34.4% (p<0.001), L-EC −29.9% (p<0.001), R-EO −48.7% (p<0.01), R-EC −39.4% (p=0.086 — borderline, not significant). Control: no changes.

*Swing performance*: Club head speed +5.73% (p<0.01), ball speed +11.3% (p<0.001), hitting efficiency +5.45% (p<0.01), driving distance +6.67% (p=0.055 — borderline). Between-group comparisons also significant for ball speed, driving distance, and hitting efficiency.

**Statistical approach**: Two-way ANOVA with independent samples design for between-group comparisons and related samples design for within-group pre-post. Results expressed as mean ± SEM. Threshold p<0.05. GraphPad Prism 10.2.2. No correction for multiple comparisons reported.

## Relevance to Trial Design

**Training duration**: 4 weeks produced substantial balance improvements (30–49% sway reduction) and measurable swing performance changes. Supports 4 weeks as a viable minimum. Longer durations would likely show continued gains but are untested here.

**Outcome measures**: mCTSIB and UST via NeuroCom are well-validated for static and unilateral balance. Neither captures CoP *timing* or weight transfer *dynamics* during the swing itself — this is the key gap between this paper and the intended trial. GCQuad is a reasonable swing performance proxy. No kinetic plate or CoP-during-swing measurement used here at all.

**Expected effect sizes**: Balance improvements are large (Cohen's d likely >1 given the % reductions and within-group SD), but these are static postural measures. Whether comparable effect sizes transfer to dynamic CoP during swing is unknown. Swing performance effects are smaller — ~6–11% — and more variable (driving distance didn't reach p<0.05 despite 6.67% change with n=16).

**Likely confounders**:
1. Training volume confound — TOGU group trained ~5×/week vs control's 2×/week; effect could partly be from additional practice time, not TOGU-specific
2. No randomisation procedure described — allocation method unclear
3. No blinding possible, but no mention of assessor blinding either
4. Single-sex, narrow age range (21±1yr), narrow handicap range — limits generalisability
5. No washout or follow-up — no durability data

---

## Limitations

**Noted by authors**: Small sample size; short intervention duration; no follow-up for long-term performance or injury outcomes; no neurological mechanism exploration.

**Additional limitations not acknowledged**:
- Training volume confound is unaddressed and is probably the most serious threat to internal validity
- No randomisation protocol described — "divided into two groups" without specifying method
- Multiple comparisons across ~20+ outcomes with no correction — inflates false positive risk
- Driving distance p=0.0553 reported in abstract as improved, which is a borderline call
- R-EC p=0.0857 described in results text as a "significant improvement" in some places — inconsistent with their own threshold
- FMS ceiling effects on right side acknowledged for hurdle step but not addressed systematically

---

## Quality Assessment

**Low–Medium**. Directionally useful, methods are reasonable, outcomes are relevant. But the training volume confound, absent randomisation description, no multiple comparison correction, and small n substantially limit causal inference. Results should be treated as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

## Flags for Trial Design

1. **Volume confound is a direct threat to your design if you replicate it.** If your training group does extra sessions, you need to either match volume in the control (active comparator) or explicitly acknowledge this as a limitation. A waitlist control with matched contact time would be stronger.

2. **This paper measures static balance, not dynamic CoP during swing.** Your trial's novelty partly rests on measuring CoP timing during the swing itself — no precedent here for expected effect sizes on that measure. You're extrapolating from static balance → dynamic swing CoP, which may not hold proportionally.

3. **The 4-week window is supported but marginal for swing outcomes.** Balance improved robustly; swing metrics were borderline on distance. If your primary outcome is CoP during swing rather than launch monitor data, the sensitivity question is open.

4. **Handicap range 15–20** is a meaningful population choice — enough skill to have a repeatable swing pattern, but enough room for improvement that ceiling effects are less likely. Worth matching if possible.
